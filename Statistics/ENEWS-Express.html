Problem Statement¶

Business Context¶

The advent of e-news, or electronic news, portals has offered us a great opportunity to quickly get updates on the day-to-day events occurring globally. The information on these portals is retrieved electronically from online databases, processed using a variety of software, and then transmitted to the users. There are multiple advantages of transmitting news electronically, like faster access to the content and the ability to utilize different technologies such as audio, graphics, video, and other interactive elements that are either not being used or aren’t common yet in traditional newspapers.

E-news Express, an online news portal, aims to expand its business by acquiring new subscribers. With every visitor to the website taking certain actions based on their interest, the company plans to analyze these actions to understand user interests and determine how to drive better engagement. The executives at E-news Express believe that there has been a decline in new monthly subscribers compared to the past year because the current webpage is not designed well enough in terms of the outline & recommended content to keep customers engaged long enough to decide to subscribe.

[Companies often analyze user responses to two variants of a product to decide which of the two variants is more effective. This experimental technique, known as A/B testing, is used to determine whether a new feature attracts users based on a chosen metric.]

Objective¶

The design team of the company has researched and created a new landing page that has a new outline & more relevant content compared to the old page. To test the effectiveness of the new landing page in gathering new subscribers, the Data Science team experimented by randomly selecting 100 users and dividing them equally into two groups. The existing landing page was served to the first group (control group) and the new landing page to the second group (treatment group). Data regarding the interaction of users in both groups with the two versions of the landing page was collected.

Being a data scientist in E-news Express, you have been asked to explore the data and perform a statistical analysis (at a significance level of 5%) to determine the effectiveness of the new landing page in gathering new subscribers for the news portal by answering the following questions:

  • Do the users spend more time on the new landing page than on the existing landing page?

  • Is the conversion rate (the proportion of users who visit the landing page and get converted) for the new page greater than the conversion rate for the old page?

  • Does the converted status depend on the preferred language?

  • Is the time spent on the new page the same for the different language users?

Observation

The new page's median (6.1 min) is clearly above the old page's (4.4 min), and its entire IQR sits above the old page's median — visually suggesting higher time spent on the new page. A formal test will confirm whether this is statistically significant.

Formulating null hypothesis¶

Let u1 = mean time spent on the new page, u2 = mean time spent on the old page.

  • H0: u1 ≤ u2 (users do not spend more time on the new page)
  • H1: u1 > u2 (users spend more time on the new page)

This is a one-tailed test, since the question asks specifically about more time, not just a difference.

Test Selection¶

An Independent Two-Sample t-test is appropriate because:

  • The response variable, time_spent_on_the_page, is continuous scale
  • The explanatory variable, landing_page, is categorical with two independent groups (old and new).
  • The objective is to compare the mean time spent between the two groups.
  • The population standard deviations are unknown. The business question is directional ("more time"), making a one-tailed t-test appropriate.

Let's test whether the t-test assumptions are satisfied or not¶

  • Continuous data — Time spent on the page is measured in minutes, which is Continuous data

  • Normally distributed populations or sample sizes > 30 — Each group has 50 observations (> 30), so by the Central Limit Theorem the distribution of sample means will be approximately normal. This is further supported by the univariate analysis, where time spent showed a symmetric, bell-shaped distribution with no outliers.

  • Independent populations — The two samples come from two different groups of users (control saw the old page, treatment saw the new page), with no user appearing in both, so the populations are independent.

  • Unknown population standard deviations σ₁ and σ₂ — We only have the sample standard deviations, not the population values. This is precisely why we use a t-test rather than a z-test.

  • Random sampling from the population — We are informed that the 100 users were randomly selected and randomly assigned to the two groups.

We have sufficient statistical evidence at a 5% significance level to conclude that users spend more time on the new landing page than on the old landing page.

Observation:

The new page converts 33 of 50 users (66%) while the old page converts 21 of 50 (42%) — a gap of 24 percentage points, visually suggesting a higher conversion rate for the new page. A formal test will confirm whether this is statistically significant.

Formulate the hypotheses¶

  • Let p1 = conversion rate of the new page, p2 = conversion rate of the old page.

  • H₀: p1 ≤ p2 (the conversion rate of the new page is not greater than that of the old page)

  • H₁: p1 > p2 (the conversion rate of the new page is greater than that of the old page)

This is a one-tailed test, since the question asks specifically whether the new rate is greater.

Test Selection¶

A Two-Proportion Z-test is appropriate because:

  • The response variable, converted, is binary (Yes/No).

  • The objective is to compare the conversion rates (proportions) of two independent groups (old and new landing pages).

  • The observations are independent because each user visited only one landing page.

  • The sample size is sufficiently large to approximate the sampling distribution of the difference in proportions using a normal distribution.

Checking the Assumptions¶

  • Binomially distributed population — Each user either converts or does not convert, so the outcome is binary (success/failure).

  • Random sampling from the population — We are informed that the 100 users were randomly selected and randomly assigned to the two groups.

  • Independent populations — The two samples come from two different groups of users (control saw the old page, treatment saw the new page), with no user in both.

  • Can the binomial distribution be approximated to normal? — For binary data, the standard check is whether np and n(1-p) are both >= 10 for each group, where n is the sample size and p is the sample proportion of conversions:

    New page: np1 = 50 x (33/50) = 33 >= 10 n(1-p1) = 50 x (17/50) = 17 >= 10

    Old page: np2 = 50 x (21/50) = 21 >= 10 n(1-p2) = 50 x (29/50) = 29 >= 10

Since all four values are >= 10, the normal approximation holds.

Buisness Implication¶

Formulate the hypotheses¶

The objective is to determine whether the conversion status is associated with the preferred language of the users.

H0 (Null Hypothesis) The converted status is independent of the preferred language.

In other words, there is no significant association between a user's preferred language and their subscription status.

H1 (Alternative Hypothesis)

The converted status depends on the preferred language.

In other words, there is a significant association between a user's preferred language and their subscription status.

Test Selection¶

  • The objective is to determine whether there is a significant association between preferred language and conversion status.Since both variables are categorical:Preferred Language has 3 categories (English, French, and Spanish). Converted has 2 categories (Yes and No).

  • Therefore, the Chi-Square Test of Independence is the appropriate statistical test to determine whether conversion status depends on the user's preferred language.

Check the assumptions for Chi Square test of independence¶

  • Categorical Variables: Both language_preferred and converted are categorical variables.

  • Independent Observations: Each user appears only once in the dataset, and the observations are independent.

  • Expected Cell Frequency: The expected frequency in each cell of the contingency table should be at least 5. This assumption will be verified using the expected frequencies returned by the chi-square test.

Since these assumptions are satisfied, the Chi-Square Test of Independence can be used to test the association between preferred language and conversion status.

Formulate the hypotheses¶

Let u1, u2, u3 = mean time spent on the new page for English, French, and Spanish users respectively.

H₀: u1 = u2 = u3 (the mean time spent on the new page is the same across all three language groups)

H₁: At least one language group's mean time spent is different from the others.

Select the appropriate test¶

A One-Way ANOVA is appropriate because:

  • The response variable, time_spent_on_the_page, is continuous.

  • The explanatory variable, language_preferred, is categorical with three independent groups (English, French, and Spanish).

  • The objective is to compare the mean time spent across more than two independent groups.

Checking the Assumptions for ANOVA test¶

Before performing the One-Way ANOVA, the following assumptions should be verified:

  • Independent Observations: Each user appears only once in the dataset and belongs to only one preferred language group. Therefore, the observations are independent.

  • Normality: The distribution of time spent within each language group should be approximately normal. This assumption will be verified using the Shapiro-Wilk test.

  • Homogeneity of Variances: The population variances of the three language groups should be approximately equal. This assumption will be verified using Levene's test.

The results of these assumption tests are presented below before performing the One-Way ANOVA.

Conclusion and Business Recommendations¶

Summary of statistical findings¶

Four hypothesis tests were performed at a 5% significance level to evaluate the effectiveness of the new landing page.

  • Q1 Do users spend more time on the new landing page than the old landing page?

    • Independent two-sample t-test (p = 0.00014): Reject the null hypothesis. Users spend significantly more time on the new landing page than on the old landing page.

  • Q2 Is the conversion rate of the new landing page greater than that of the old landing page?

    • Two-proportion Z-test (p = 0.008): Reject the null hypothesis. The new landing page has a significantly higher conversion rate.

  • Q3 Does conversion status depend on preferred language?

    • Chi-Square Test of Independence (p = 0.213): Fail to reject the null hypothesis. There is no statistically significant association between preferred language and conversion status.

  • Q4 – Is the mean time spent on the new landing page the same across different language groups?

    • One-Way ANOVA (p = 0.432): Fail to reject the null hypothesis. There is no statistically significant difference in the mean time spent among the English, French, and Spanish language groups.

Key conclusions¶

  • The new landing page significantly improves user engagement. Users spend an average of 6.22 minutes on the new page compared to 4.53 minutes on the old page, representing an increase of approximately 37%.

  • The new landing page also significantly improves subscriber conversion. The conversion rate increased from 42% on the old landing page to 66% on the new landing page, an improvement of 24 percentage points.

  • Preferred language does not have a statistically significant impact on conversion. Although English-speaking users exhibited a higher conversion rate in the sample, the observed differences were not statistically significant.

  • Preferred language also does not have a statistically significant impact on user engagement. The mean time spent on the new landing page is comparable across English, French, and Spanish users.

Business recommendations¶

  1. Roll out the new landing page to all users. The evidence is strong and consistent on the two metrics that matter most — engagement and conversion — so E-news Express should replace the old page with the new one across the board.

  2. Do not invest in language-specific versions of the page. Since neither conversion nor time spent depends on preferred language, building and maintaining separate pages per language would add cost without a measurable return. The single new design serves all language groups equally well.

  3. Keep the content strategy that drives engagement. The new page's improved outline and more relevant content appear to be what hold users' attention longer and lead to more subscriptions. Future design iterations should preserve and build on these strengths.

  4. Continue Monitoring Performance After Rollout Although the experimental results are statistically significant, the study was conducted on a sample of 100 users. After deploying the new landing page, E-News Express should continue monitoring engagement and conversion metrics on a larger user base to ensure that the observed improvements are sustained under real-world conditions

  5. Consider further experiments to optimize further. A/B testing has proven valuable here. The company could continue testing specific elements — headlines, layout, calls-to-action, recommended-content placement — to keep improving conversion beyond the gains already achieved.
